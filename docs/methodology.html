How the models work

Page last updated 27 September 2026. Live figures below update with the site's data.

Before you read the numbers

  • Predictions are probabilities, not guarantees. A 60% chance means the other thing happens about four times in ten.
  • Disagreeing with the bookmakers is not proof of value. So far the bookmakers have been more accurate than our model (see below).
  • Predicted line-ups are guesses. Managers rotate, and late injuries and illness aren't always reported.
  • Data can be incomplete or out of date. Injury lists are missing for some leagues, and results, odds and news arrive with a delay.
  • Past performance doesn't guarantee future performance.

Nothing on this site is betting advice. The Paper Simulation uses no real money.

How fresh is the data?

Loading…

  • Every night (about 03:00 UK time in winter, 04:00 in summer): results, fixtures, match statistics, injury lists and odds are refreshed, then the ratings and predictions are rebuilt.
  • On match days, every 30 minutes: for matches starting within 3 hours, odds and injury lists are refreshed and those matches are re-predicted.
  • Fantasy Premier League data is refreshed three times a day.

Our data comes from API-Football and the Fantasy Premier League game. If they're late, wrong or missing something, so are we. Known gaps: injury lists only feed the predictions in 10 leagues, and many lower-league clubs have none at all. xG only exists for about a third to a half of matches, mostly in the bigger leagues. Odds only appear about two weeks before a match. If a scheduled update fails, the site keeps showing the last good data rather than half-updated data, so a date above that looks old means an update is overdue.

What the numbers mean

Club Current Strength

A club's level right now, on an Elo-style scale where 100 points is worth about one goal a game. After every match, both clubs move by how much better or worse they did than expected. When a match has xG, the "result" is mostly xG (70%) with a bit of the actual score (30%), because xG is less affected by luck. Big wins are capped so one freak score can't swing a rating.

Club Baseline Strength

A club's long-term level: an average of its Current Strength over roughly its last 100 matches, leaning a little towards the more recent ones. It moves slowly, and it's what the club rankings are sorted by. Current vs Baseline shows whether a club is playing above or below its usual level.

Nation Strength

The Nations tab runs the club model over every men's full international since 1872, from the public international_results dataset. The scale is the same (100 points is about one goal a game), but home advantage (half a goal) only counts away from neutral grounds, big wins are capped at 8 goals rather than 3, and there's no xG. Nations are ranked by Current Strength, with its change over the last 12 months. Only FIFA members that have played in the last four years are listed.

Recent Movement

How much a club's Current Strength has changed over its last 6 matches, and over the last 30 days. It describes what has happened, not what will happen. It's flagged when a club hasn't played for over 45 days.

Player Ability

A 0–100 estimate of a player's level, where an average Premier League regular is about 75. It starts from the strength of the clubs he has been a regular for, then moves up or down by up to about 15 points depending on how his stats compare with other players in the same position: tackles and duels for defenders, passing and chance creation for midfielders, shots and goals for forwards, and so on. The stats used are the ones that repeat from season to season rather than luck. Players with few minutes are pulled down until they've played more.

It only covers leagues with match-by-match player data, and it can't see things the data doesn't record, like off-the-ball work or a player carrying an injury.

Match probabilities

For each match the model works out how many goals each side is likely to score, from:

  • the two clubs' strength (Current and Baseline, leaning more on Current for matches close to kick-off)
  • home advantage, and each club's own home and away record
  • their attacking and defensive tendencies over the last 12 months, using xG where it exists
  • known injuries and suspensions (in the 10 leagues with reliable injury history)
  • the predicted line-ups (a small effect)

Those expected goals are turned into win, draw and loss chances, with a small boost to the draw in close games. The model doesn't use bookmakers' odds.

Projected xG

The number of goals the model expects each side to score, such as 1.6–0.9. It's an average over many possible games, not a score prediction: a side projected 1.6 often scores 0, 1 or 3.

Predicted line-ups

Predicted line-ups are built from who has been starting and playing minutes recently, with players we know to be injured or suspended left out. It's a guess made before the team sheet is out, and it can be wrong when a manager rotates, a player is ill or news breaks late. Our injury lists only include what's been reported.

Model vs Market

We compare the model's chances with the bookmakers' chances: their odds with the bookmaker's margin taken out, averaged across bookmakers. A big difference is a disagreement, not an edge. The bookmakers see team news, sharp bettors' money and information we don't have, and so far they've been right more often when we disagreed (see below).

Two kinds of evidence

We keep these strictly apart, and every figure on this page is labelled with one of them:

Live record
Predictions saved before kick-off, in a store that can't be edited afterwards. This is the only honest test of how the model does on matches it hasn't seen. We only started saving these on 26 September 2026, so the sample is still small.
Historical test
The model re-run on past matches, using only what was known before each one. It's useful for choosing settings, but it isn't proof: the model was built by people who had already seen those seasons, and some past inputs have been rebuilt with today's data. Treat it as an upper bound on what to expect.

The bookmakers were more accurate. The gap is small and the sample is short, so it can't rule out the model being as good, but there's no evidence it's better. In the 82 matches where the model and the bookmakers differed by 10 points or more on a result, the model gave its side a 42% chance on average, the bookmakers 30%, and it happened 29% of the time. Every individual bookmaker we checked was also more accurate than the model. That's why a disagreement on the Model vs Market tab is not a tip.

Historical test What line-ups are worth

Across 9,601 past matches from July 2024 on, using our predicted XIs made the match probabilities a little more accurate than ignoring line-ups. Swapping in the actual team sheets improved them only slightly more. For match results, knowing the exact XI matters less than you might think, partly because much of what a line-up tells you is already in the club's rating.

How the club and player ratings were checked

Historical test These ratings don't predict a single outcome we can score each week, so they're checked through what they're used for:

  • Club strength is what drives the match probabilities above. The Current / Baseline mix used for matches (60/40 near kick-off) scored best of the mixes tried, in both 2024/25 and 2025 onwards.
  • Player Ability repeats from one season to the next better than the earlier version did (rank correlation 0.54, and 0.44 for players who changed club). Versions of the ratings with club strength taken out did no better at predicting players' later performances, and did worse at predicting match results.

We only change a model when a test shows a clear improvement on matches it wasn't tuned on. In September 2026 four proposed changes were tested on past seasons this way. None cleared the bar, so none were made.

The measures, in plain words

Most likely result was right
How often the result the model rated most likely actually happened. Easy to read, but it ignores how confident the model was, and about half is typical for football.
Log loss
Punishes the model for being confident and wrong. Lower is better. Giving every result a one-in-three chance scores 1.099. Football is hard to predict, so even the bookmakers score well above 0.9.
Brier score
The average squared gap between the chances given and what happened (here summed over home, draw and away, so it runs from 0 to 2). Lower is better. One-in-three for everything scores 0.667.
Calibration
Whether "30%" really means 30%: of all the things the model gave about a 30% chance, roughly 30% should happen.
Sample size
How many matches a figure is based on. A few hundred matches can swing a lot through luck alone. Thousands are needed before small differences mean much.
