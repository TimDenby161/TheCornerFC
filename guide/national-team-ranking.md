# National team ranking

`thecornerfc/models/nations.py` runs the club ranking's Elo over every men's full international since 1872, for the **Nations** tab (`.export/nations.json`). The formula is the same (100 points = one goal, capped goal difference, `rank_change = (act_diff - exp_diff) * K`), with these differences, tuned by backtest in `experiments/nations_elo`:
- Home advantage is 50 points, and only applies away from neutral venues.
- Goal difference is capped at 8, not 3.
- There's no xG.

Friendlies, qualifiers and tournament finals all count the same. Weighting them differently scored slightly worse, but `TIER_WEIGHT` is there if that changes.

**Data.** The results come from the public [international_results](https://github.com/martj42/international_results) dataset, which has a neutral-venue flag and the tournament for every match. It is downloaded to `.cache/international_results.csv` and refreshed when it's more than 20 hours old. A failed download keeps the cached copy. Nothing goes in the database. The export rebuilds the ranking every night, and it isn't critical: if it fails, the last `nations.json` stays. To rebuild only this file: `python -m thecornerfc nations` (safe in local mode).

Which teams are listed:
- Only FIFA members appear: nations that have played a World Cup qualifier since 2010, which is 211 teams.
- Of those, only nations that have played in the last four years are listed.
- Non-FIFA sides such as Jersey still count as opponents.
- Tournaments for non-FIFA sides (CONIFA, Island Games) and multi-sport games (mostly under-23 teams) are left out.

**API-Football (ready, off).** The dataset usually has results within a few days. API-Football can fill that gap and later supply upcoming fixtures:
1. Run `db/migrations/20260929_national_fixtures.sql`, then `db/migrations/20260929_national_lineups.sql`, then `db/migrations/20260929_national_players.sql`.
2. Run `python -m thecornerfc sync national`. By default it pulls the current season of each competition in `config.NATIONAL_TEAM_LEAGUES`, at about 20–40 calls, and logs each competition's name so you can check the ids. It then fetches the line-ups of every finished match it has (see below).
3. Set `THECORNERFC_NATIONAL_SYNC=true` in the nightly workflow to refresh them every night.

These matches go in their own table, `national_fixtures`, never in `fixtures`. The club ranking, predictions and site export read every row of `fixtures`, so national teams there would enter the club Elo.

**Projections and scores.** `thecornerfc/models/national_predictions.py` projects every national team match that hasn't kicked off, so the Matches tab shows internationals as it shows club matches: projected goals, likely score, win/draw/loss chances, over 2.5 and both to score, key reasons, model detail, and a 1–5 rating once the match is finished. The expected margin is the national ranking's rank gap plus its home advantage (half a goal, none at a neutral ground: `national_fixtures.neutral`, or a tournament's finals where that is null). `predictions.project` turns it into goals from what each of two level sides scores (`LEVEL_GOALS`, 1.1), and the chances come from the club functions unchanged. Backtest in `experiments/nations_proj`: on 2014–26, W/D/L log loss 0.8703 against 1.0522 for guessing the base rates. Left out, because national teams have no data for them: Baseline Strength, attack/defence tendencies, injury lists, predicted line-up ratings and bookmakers' odds (so no Model vs Market, paper bets or Stats tab entries). Rows go in `national_fixture_predictions` (`db/migrations/20261007_national_predictions.sql`), never `fixture_predictions`, and a match's row is left alone once it has kicked off. The nightly run makes them after the club projections; a failure there is logged and never stops the run. Matches between sides the ranking doesn't know (youth teams, clubs) get none. A finished match from the last 21 days with no projection (played before one could be made) gets one afterwards from the ranks both sides had going into it (`backfill_predictions`), as club matches do; its row was written after kickoff, so the export marks it `backfill` and the site says it was reconstructed. The match-day run refreshes the scores of internationals that kicked off in the last five hours, as it does for club matches (`matchday.refresh_national_results`); the site shows them from the next export.

**Line-ups.** `ingest.sync_national_lineups` stores each finished international's starting XIs, formations and coaches the same way `sync_cup_lineups` does for clubs: one `/fixtures?ids=` call per 20 matches, each starter's grid and role, and the coach on the line-up. They go in `national_fixture_lineups` and `national_fixture_formations`, not the club tables, which the club models read in full. `national_fixtures.players_fetched_at` marks a match done: once its line-ups arrive, or after the retry window if API-Football never has them. Player ids are API-Football's, so they join to `players`. It runs after the fixtures in `sync national` and in the nightly run. For older matches, pull their seasons first (`python -m thecornerfc sync national --seasons 2022 2023 2024 2025`); the next line-up run picks them up.

**Predicted XIs, odds and paper bets (since 2026-10-07).** `thecornerfc/models/national_lineups.py` picks each national team's XI for a match that hasn't kicked off: the club predictor's slot filling on the team's last five national matches (minutes × role fit), with no squad list, injury list or player ranks, so it names fewer starters than a club's. It's captured in `lineup_prediction_snapshots` under its own line-up model version, shown on the match card (it travels with the match's model detail) and scored in the Line-up record's live record against the official XI `sync_national_lineups` now records. `evaluation.load_lineups` leaves national XIs out unless asked, so the Methodology page and the club protocols stay club-only; the reconstructed history has none. `ingest.sync_national_odds` collects bookmakers' odds nightly for the national competitions with a match in the next 14 days, and the match-day run refreshes them near kick-off. With them, the match cards and the Stats tab compare the national projection with the market, and the paper simulation bets on internationals in the same bank (`betting.py`: the bet's `league_id` is the competition and its only match tag is `intl`). Each nightly projection is stored as a `match_prediction_snapshots` row under the `national-match-prediction` version, which is what a paper decision points to; everything that scores the club model reads that table joined to `fixtures`, so a national row never enters it. Bets settle on the 90-minute score (`db/migrations/20261007_national_ft_scores.sql`; until it is applied, a bet on a match that went to extra time is void). The Stats tab counts finished internationals with the club matches, under their own competitions (`export.national_stat_rows`).

**Stat lines and nation pages.** The same calls give each player's stat line (minutes, goals, assists, rating, cards, as `fixture_players` has for clubs), kept in `national_fixture_players` with the player's name as sent (not every international is in `players`), and the coach's name on `national_fixture_formations`. A match is marked done once both line-ups and stat lines arrive, or after the retry window. The `national_players` migration sets matches fetched before it to be fetched once more (about 8 calls for the current seasons). The export writes `.export/nations/<team id>.json` for each national team with a finished match in the last four years: its matches with formation and coach, and every appearance (stat lines, plus starters with none, whose minutes are unknown). `nations.json` gives each ranked nation its `team_id`. The nation page's Formations and Players tabs read these files. `ingest.sync_national_coaches` (in `sync national` and the nightly run) stores each national team's head coach and the date he started in `team_coaches`, as `sync_coaches` does for clubs (one `/coachs?team=` call per team that played in the last year, rechecked weekly); the tabs cover the matches since then.

How API-Football matches are merged:
- They're added only where the dataset doesn't have the match (the same two nations within a day), so the dataset's neutral flags win.
- Tournament finals are assumed to be neutral.
- Team names are mapped with `nations.API_NAMES`. A name that doesn't map is logged and its matches skipped, because an unknown name would start a phantom nation at 1000.
